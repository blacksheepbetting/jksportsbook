BK’s Last Leg: NBA Power Rankings — The Inaugural Edition

BK sorts all 30 NBA teams into five tiers, weighing each franchise’s championship outlook against the futures market.

Brandon “BK” Klein / 21 min read / November 20, 2026

30 teams. Five tiers. Zero participation trophies.

The NBA standings tell you who's winning. They don't always tell you who's actually dangerous.

A great record can hide an easy schedule, a mediocre record can disguise a team with an injury list longer than a CVS receipt, and a championship banner doesn't mean the current roster deserves respect.

Welcome to the inaugural edition of BK's Last Leg NBA Power Rankings, where we're separating all 30 teams into five tiers, from championship contenders to franchises that should probably start scouting next year's draft class.

Some placements will be obvious. Others might make you question whether I actually watch basketball. That's fine, provoking super fans is half the fun.

How we're evaluating teams

We're looking beyond win-loss records, considering recent performance, strength of schedule, offensive and defensive efficiency, net rating, roster talent, depth, injuries, and championship experience.

We're also comparing each team's championship outlook against Pinnacle's futures market. Being a championship contender doesn't automatically make a team a good bet. Sometimes the price is right, and sometimes you're paying for a famous jersey and a prayer.

The numbers provide the evidence. My basketball judgment determines what they mean.

One thing before we get started: There's no particular order within each tier. These aren't rankings from 1 to 30; this is an assessment of where each franchise belongs in the NBA hierarchy.

And if your favorite team landed somewhere you don't like, don't worry. I'm sure your mom or grandma would love to hear about it.

Now, let's separate the contenders from the pretenders.

TIER 1: CHAMPIONSHIP CONTENDERS

The teams with the production, talent, and overall makeup to realistically compete for an NBA championship.

San Antonio Spurs (13–3)

Victor Wembanyama and the Spurs apparently missed the memo about young teams needing to wait their turn. San Antonio has paired an elite defense with one of the league's most impressive scoring differentials, making its championship ambitions impossible to ignore. The postseason will test their experience, but the talent and production already belong among the NBA's best.

PINNACLE +306 | IMPLIED 24.63% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: San Antonio deserves championship respect, but nearly a 25% implied probability is a steep price for a young roster still facing questions about deep-playoff experience. Love the team. Not the number.

Houston Rockets (12–5)

Houston isn't simply collecting wins; it's making a convincing argument that it belongs among the NBA's elite. The Rockets have established themselves as one of the league's most dangerous offensive teams, pairing impressive scoring production with a roster capable of creating problems for opponents on both ends of the floor. What makes Houston particularly interesting is its versatility. The Rockets aren't dependent on one specific style of basketball to succeed. They have the personnel to adjust to different matchups, the offensive firepower to overwhelm opponents, and enough defensive flexibility to make life difficult for opposing stars. There are still questions about whether this group can translate its regular-season success into a deep postseason run, but those concerns shouldn't overshadow what Houston has already demonstrated. This isn't some cute November surprise. Houston looks like a legitimate championship contender.

PINNACLE +1211 | IMPLIED 7.63% | POTENTIAL VALUE

BK's betting take: Houston is playing like a championship contender without carrying a championship-favorite price tag. At a little over 7.6% implied probability, the Rockets offer one of the more interesting opportunities on the board. The market might not be completely sold yet, but I'm certainly paying attention.

Boston Celtics (11–4)

Boston has one of the NBA's most impressive statistical profiles, combining outstanding defensive production with a double-digit average scoring margin. Add a championship-tested foundation, and the Celtics have earned the benefit of the doubt. A couple of disappointing nights don't suddenly turn a legitimate contender into a fraud.

PINNACLE +808 | IMPLIED 11.01% | FAIR PRICE

BK’s betting take: Boston has the efficiency and experience to justify serious respect from the market. There's championship potential here, but the price already recognizes plenty of it.

Minnesota Timberwolves (11–5)

Minnesota has built its identity around making opponents regret showing up to work. The Timberwolves' elite defensive production and impressive scoring differential give them a reliable foundation for postseason success. Pretty basketball is optional when you can consistently make the other team miserable.

PINNACLE +1528 | IMPLIED 6.14% | POTENTIAL VALUE

BK’s betting take: An elite defense and a legitimate championship profile at roughly a 6% implied probability? That's a price worth examining. Minnesota doesn't need to win a beauty contest to cash a futures ticket.

Philadelphia 76ers (13–3)

Philadelphia's record is prettier than its underlying numbers, and a schedule softer than LeBron deserves some of the credit. But when your roster features Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown, and LeBron James, supported by serious depth and championship experience, you can afford a few statistical imperfections. The Sixers have enough talent to win in several different ways, and that versatility matters considerably more in May than winning every November game by 20.

PINNACLE +796 | IMPLIED 11.16% | POTENTIAL VALUE

BK’s betting take: The schedule hasn’t exactly been a gauntlet, but the roster certainly isn’t lacking firepower. Philadelphia has the talent, depth, and championship-tested personnel to justify a serious look at this price, even if the efficiency numbers haven't been spectacular.

TIER 2: DARK HORSE CONTENDERS

Teams capable of making a serious postseason run, but with enough unanswered questions to keep them outside the championship favorites.

Detroit Pistons (10–5)

Detroit looks fantastic on paper, and victories over quality opponents suggest the success is legitimate. Unfortunately, the Pistons have also demonstrated a frustrating tendency to play down to their competition. This is a team that earned the East's top seed last season before Cleveland sent it home in Game 7 of the second round. The young core has already proven it can win regular-season games, but championship basketball demands something more: maturity, consistency, and reliable secondary scoring when opponents start making adjustments. Detroit has the talent to make serious postseason noise. Whether it has the discipline to survive four playoff rounds is another question entirely.

PINNACLE +1914 | IMPLIED 4.97% | FAIR PRICE

BK's betting take: Detroit has earned its place in the conversation, but the market is asking us to trust a core that hasn't yet delivered a deep playoff run. The price is reasonable; the postseason questions are still expensive.

Memphis Grizzlies (12–4)

Memphis is scoring like somebody accidentally left the difficulty setting on rookie mode. The Grizzlies are averaging more than 122 points per game and winning by impressive margins. The problem? They've faced one of the league's softest early schedules. The offense looks terrifying, but let's see what happens when the competition starts punching back.

PINNACLE +15633 | IMPLIED 0.64% | POTENTIAL VALUE

BK’s betting take: The soft schedule is a legitimate concern, but pricing a 12–4 team with this kind of offensive production as a near-impossible championship long shot feels aggressive. It's a speculative ticket, but the payout deserves a second look.

Portland Trail Blazers (10–5)

Portland might be the league's most interesting surprise, and unlike some early overachievers, the Trail Blazers haven't exactly been handed a cupcake schedule. Their impressive scoring differential suggests the winning is legitimate. The question is whether this relatively inexperienced group can maintain that success when opponents start treating them like a threat instead of an easy night.

PINNACLE +3022 | IMPLIED 3.20% | FAIR PRICE

BK’s betting take: Portland's record and schedule give the Trail Blazers credibility, but championship experience remains a major unknown. Interesting team, reasonable price, and not quite enough evidence to call it a bargain.

Oklahoma City Thunder (10–6)

Oklahoma City has the talent, defensive foundation, and roster depth to compete with almost anybody in the NBA. Unfortunately, having the ingredients of a championship contender doesn't automatically mean you're serving championship-caliber basketball. The Thunder have shown flashes of dominance, but their inconsistency against quality opponents raises legitimate questions about whether they're performing at the level their reputation suggests. There's no denying the ceiling. But at 10–6, Oklahoma City hasn't done enough to convince me it belongs alongside the league's most complete teams.

PINNACLE +505 | IMPLIED 16.53% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK's betting take: Pinnacle is pricing Oklahoma City like a championship favorite, but the Thunder haven't consistently played like one. I'm not paying premium prices for potential when the results haven't justified the hype.

New York Knicks (9–6)

The defending champions have earned some respect, and their positive scoring differential suggests they're still capable of making serious postseason noise. But a championship banner doesn't come with a complimentary top-tier ranking. New York has the experience and physicality to beat anyone; it simply hasn't played consistently enough to deserve a place among the favorites.

PINNACLE +1059 | IMPLIED 8.63% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: The market is giving New York plenty of credit for its championship pedigree. Fair enough, but I'd prefer to see the current Knicks consistently resemble the defending champions before paying that price.

Los Angeles Lakers (14–3)

Luka Dončić has been putting up numbers that belong in a video game, and the Lakers have been cashing in on his brilliance. At 14–3, Los Angeles has one of the NBA's most impressive records. But there's a difference between collecting wins and convincing people you're built to win a championship. A favorable schedule, defensive inconsistencies, and an offense heavily dependent on Luka's creation make the Lakers less intimidating than their record suggests. Austin Reaves provides legitimate support, but championship basketball shouldn't require your best player to perform a magic trick every night. And if you're wondering why I'm not completely sold, consider this: the Lakers needed a 106–104 escape against a Sacramento team with just one win. If you're barely surviving the league's bottom feeders, what happens when you're facing elite competition in a seven-game series? The Lakers are dangerous. Nobody is questioning that. But their record has been considerably more convincing than their overall performance.

PINNACLE +2316 | IMPLIED 4.14% | FAIR PRICE

BK's betting take: Luka makes almost anything possible, and the payout acknowledges the Lakers' weaknesses. But trusting one superstar to solve every problem across four playoff rounds is a pretty ambitious investment strategy.

TIER 3: PLAYOFF CONTENDERS

Teams capable of earning playoff berths and making life difficult for stronger opponents, but lacking the current production to justify championship expectations.

Denver Nuggets (9–5)

Denver might be the NBA’s most confusing team. The Nuggets have beaten Boston and Houston, two teams in our championship tier, yet all five of their losses have come by at least eight points, with an average losing margin of 12.4. That’s not exactly the consistency you expect from a championship contender. The talent and experience are undeniable, and on their best nights Denver looks capable of beating anybody. On their worst nights, they look like a team that forgot it was supposed to be competing for a title. Denver has demonstrated its ceiling. Now it needs to demonstrate its floor isn’t somewhere in the basement.

PINNACLE +2820 | IMPLIED 3.42% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: Two impressive victories don’t erase five convincing defeats. The Nuggets have championship pedigree, but I’m not paying for their best-case scenario when their worst-case performances are still showing up this regularly.

Cleveland Cavaliers (9–8)

Cleveland is the basketball equivalent of a car that looks great in the driveway but keeps stalling at red lights. The Cavaliers have enough talent to compete with quality opponents, and their positive scoring differential suggests they're better than their 9–8 record. Unfortunately, the standings don't award bonus points for looking promising. Cleveland has struggled to establish the consistency expected from a serious Eastern Conference threat. The talent is there, the potential is obvious, and the results remain frustratingly ordinary. At some point, being better than your record needs to translate into actually improving it.

PINNACLE +2920 | IMPLIED 3.31% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK's betting take: Cleveland has the talent to make this number interesting, but we're talking about winning a championship, not sneaking into the playoffs. The Cavaliers need to demonstrate more before I'd put money behind that outcome.

Atlanta Hawks (10–6)

Atlanta has quietly put together a respectable start, including an impressive victory over Oklahoma City. The Hawks can compete with quality opponents, but their relatively modest scoring margin suggests they're walking a tightrope most nights. They're dangerous enough to spoil somebody's postseason plans, even if they aren't ready to make championship plans of their own.

PINNACLE +7543 | IMPLIED 1.31% | SPECULATIVE

BK’s betting take: The payout is tempting, but there's a big difference between upsetting a contender and becoming one. Atlanta could ruin somebody's playoff bracket without getting anywhere near the Larry O'Brien Trophy.

Miami Heat (9–6)

Miami has caught fire recently, stringing together six consecutive victories and knocking off Houston along the way. The Heat are playing with confidence, but their overall scoring differential suggests the margin for error remains thin. Nobody wants to deal with Miami when it's rolling. That doesn't mean I'm ready to book its Finals hotel.

PINNACLE +4027 | IMPLIED 2.42% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: Miami's recent form deserves attention, but the price asks us to believe a hot streak can turn into a championship run. I'm not confusing a dangerous playoff opponent with a team likely to win the whole thing.

Dallas Mavericks (8–7)

Dallas has enough offensive talent to make opponents uncomfortable, but its negative scoring differential suggests the Mavericks aren't exactly returning the favor defensively. This team can compete when the offense gets going. Unfortunately, playoff basketball tends to expose teams that treat defense like an optional subscription service.

PINNACLE +18080 | IMPLIED 0.55% | SPECULATIVE

BK’s betting take: At these odds, Dallas doesn't need to be a favorite to be interesting. But the defensive concerns make a four-round playoff run difficult to imagine. The payout is enormous for a reason.

Phoenix Suns (8–7)

Phoenix has demonstrated enough offensive firepower to challenge quality opponents, including an impressive victory over Denver. Unfortunately, the Suns have also spent plenty of time looking like a team still trying to figure out exactly what it wants to be. At 8–7, Phoenix has the talent to make the playoffs but hasn't established the consistency necessary to threaten the NBA's elite. The scoring potential is intriguing. The overall results are considerably less convincing. The Suns can be dangerous on any given night. The problem is figuring out which version of Phoenix is actually going to show up.

PINNACLE +50077 | IMPLIED 0.20% | SPECULATIVE

BK's betting take: This is the kind of payout that makes you stare at the screen twice. Phoenix has enough talent to make the number intriguing, but there's a reason Pinnacle isn't losing sleep over a Suns championship.

TIER 4: PLAY-IN / FRINGE TEAMS

Teams with a realistic chance to compete for a postseason opportunity, but enough flaws to make a guaranteed playoff berth questionable.

Orlando Magic (7–8)

Orlando has the defensive personnel to frustrate almost anybody. Unfortunately, basketball still requires putting the ball through the hoop. The Magic's offensive struggles and negative scoring differential have turned an intriguing young roster into an inconsistent one. You can only ask your defense to carry so much weight before somebody needs to help with the scoring.

PINNACLE +8045 | IMPLIED 1.23% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: Orlando's defense gives it a fighting chance on any given night. But winning a championship requires four playoff series, and the Magic haven't shown enough offensively to make that a convincing proposition.

Toronto Raptors (6–10)

Toronto might be one of the NBA's more frustrating teams to evaluate. The Raptors' overall scoring differential is practically even, suggesting they're considerably more competitive than their 6–10 record indicates. They've demonstrated the ability to challenge quality opponents, but turning competitive performances into actual victories has become a recurring problem. There's clearly more talent here than the standings suggest. Unfortunately, being competitive and being good aren't always the same thing. Toronto doesn't need another moral victory. It needs to figure out how to finish games.

PINNACLE +1814 | IMPLIED 5.22% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK's betting take: Pinnacle is pricing Toronto like a legitimate dark horse while we're still waiting for the Raptors to string together consistent victories. A 5.22% implied championship probability? Somebody sees something I don't.

Indiana Pacers (5–11)

Indiana has struggled to establish consistency on either end, with defensive problems and uneven offensive execution dragging down its record. The Pacers can compete on any given night, but that isn't particularly reassuring when the other nights keep ending in losses. The play-in race looks like a more realistic destination than a comfortable playoff berth.

PINNACLE +5032 | IMPLIED 1.95% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: Indiana hasn't demonstrated enough to justify serious championship consideration. Even at this payout, I'd rather see the Pacers establish themselves as a playoff team before discussing them as a championship investment.

LA Clippers (4–10)

The Clippers have spent much of the early season defending like they're trying to avoid getting too close to anybody. A significant negative scoring differential reflects a team regularly being outplayed rather than simply suffering from bad luck. There's still talent on the roster, but talent that doesn't produce wins is just an expensive collection of names.

PINNACLE +15529 | IMPLIED 0.64% | SPECULATIVE

BK’s betting take: The odds are enormous, but so is the gap between the Clippers' talent on paper and their actual results. I'm not interested in betting on a theoretical version of a team that hasn't shown up yet.

Golden State Warriors (8–8)

Golden State deserves some sympathy after navigating one of the league's toughest schedules while dealing with injuries to major contributors, including Stephen Curry and Jimmy Butler. But sympathy doesn't count in the standings. A five-game losing streak and negative scoring differential leave the Warriors looking like a team fighting to stay relevant rather than one preparing for another championship run. The ceiling is still interesting; the current reality is considerably less glamorous.

PINNACLE +4528 | IMPLIED 2.16% | OVERPRICED LEAN

BK’s betting take: A healthy Warriors roster could make this number interesting, but Pinnacle is still charging for a comeback story that hasn't happened. Until Golden State demonstrates it can stay healthy and consistently win, I'm not paying for nostalgia.

TIER 5: LOTTERY TEAMS

Teams whose current performance suggests the NBA Draft Lottery is a more realistic destination than meaningful postseason basketball.

Chicago Bulls (4–11)

Chicago has occasionally demonstrated it can score. Unfortunately, its opponents seem to have received the same invitation. The Bulls are allowing far too many points, and their ugly scoring differential reflects a defense that has become a liability. Until somebody decides to guard someone, postseason dreams should probably remain dreams.

PINNACLE +14128 | IMPLIED 0.70% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: The odds look enormous until you remember Chicago would have to win four playoff series. I'd settle for seeing the Bulls defend four consecutive possessions first.

New Orleans Pelicans (5–12)

New Orleans has enough individual talent to convince people it should be better than this. The scoreboard, however, remains unconvinced. A severely negative scoring differential and persistent defensive problems have made the Pelicans one of the league's more disappointing teams. Talent is nice. Playing winning basketball would be nicer.

PINNACLE +12462 | IMPLIED 0.80% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: New Orleans has the talent to occasionally look dangerous, but occasional flashes don't justify a championship wager. The Pelicans have far too much to fix before this becomes a serious conversation.

Washington Wizards (6–11)

Washington has managed a few victories, but its overall performance suggests a team still searching for a reliable foundation. Defensive issues and inconsistent offensive production have left the Wizards looking much closer to a rebuilding project than a playoff contender. The future might be promising. The present could use some work.

PINNACLE +50077 | IMPLIED 0.20% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: A massive payout doesn't automatically create value. Washington needs to establish a winning foundation before anybody should seriously entertain the idea of a championship run.

Charlotte Hornets (4–11)

Charlotte's scoring differential is so ugly it should probably come with a warning label. The Hornets have struggled to score efficiently while routinely surrendering far too much defensively. There's potential within the roster, but potential doesn't help much when opponents are consistently running you out of the building.

PINNACLE +21087 | IMPLIED 0.47% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: The payout is astronomical, but Charlotte hasn't shown anything resembling a championship foundation. Some lottery tickets have better odds of making you happy.

Brooklyn Nets (3–12)

Brooklyn appears to be treating this season like an extended job interview. The Nets are still evaluating their roster, searching for consistency, and attempting to establish something resembling a defensive identity. There may be useful pieces for the future, but the present isn't offering much reason to start shopping for playoff tickets.

PINNACLE +14128 | IMPLIED 0.70% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: Brooklyn is still figuring out what it wants to become. Betting on the Nets to win a championship before they've established a competitive identity feels a little premature.

Utah Jazz (3–14)

Utah has shown it can occasionally surprise somebody, including an impressive victory over the Lakers. Unfortunately, three wins in 17 games suggest those surprises aren't happening nearly often enough. The Jazz might steal a few games from unsuspecting opponents, but they're not stealing anybody's playoff spot at this rate.

PINNACLE +16210 | IMPLIED 0.61% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: Utah can surprise a contender for one night. Asking the Jazz to win 16 playoff games is an entirely different proposition. I'll pass.

Sacramento Kings (1–14)

Sacramento's scoring differential is the kind of number that makes you wonder whether the Kings are participating in the same league as everyone else. They've struggled on both ends, and the results have been nothing short of disastrous. The draft lottery isn't looming on the horizon. It's practically sending Sacramento a calendar invitation.

PINNACLE +17952 | IMPLIED 0.55% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: You could add another zero to those odds and I'd still have questions. Sacramento needs to figure out how to win its second regular-season game before we discuss winning 16 playoff games.

Milwaukee Bucks (1–15)

Milwaukee has managed to turn an entire opening month into a cautionary tale. Twelve consecutive losses and one of the league's worst scoring differentials have buried whatever optimism existed entering the season. At this point, the Bucks don't need a motivational speech. They need wins, preferably several of them.

PINNACLE +16210 | IMPLIED 0.61% | NO VALUE LEAN

BK’s betting take: At 1–15, Milwaukee has much bigger problems than whether its championship odds are attractive. I'd like to see the Bucks win two consecutive games before discussing their chances of winning four playoff series.

THE LAST LEG

November doesn't hand out championship trophies, but it does offer a pretty good opportunity to identify who's playing legitimate basketball and who's surviving on reputation.

Some teams have earned their place through dominant performances. Others have enough talent and experience to make their statistical shortcomings worth overlooking.

And then there are the teams still expecting us to respect what they accomplished two or three seasons ago.

Sorry, but yesterday's championship doesn't win tomorrow's game.

The betting market offers another perspective. Sometimes Pinnacle agrees with our assessment. Other times, the odds suggest a team deserves far more respect than we've given it—or that the market might be overlooking a legitimate contender.

Houston and Minnesota look particularly interesting at their prices. Memphis offers an enormous payout for a team playing impressive basketball, although its schedule and postseason questions make it a speculative proposition.

Meanwhile, Oklahoma City and Toronto are receiving considerably more championship respect from Pinnacle than their current performances have earned in these rankings.

That doesn't mean the market is necessarily wrong. It means there's an argument worth having.

These rankings will change as the season progresses. Injuries will reshape rotations, trades will change expectations, and somebody currently sitting in Tier 4 might eventually make me look like an idiot.

That's fine. I've been called worse.

But one thing won't change: these rankings aren't about rewarding famous jerseys, protecting popular narratives, or making every fan base feel appreciated.

They're about evaluating what teams have shown us, questioning what they haven't, and occasionally reminding people that their favorite franchise isn't nearly as impressive as they think.

Reputation might get you into the conversation. But around here, you've still got to earn your seat at the table.
