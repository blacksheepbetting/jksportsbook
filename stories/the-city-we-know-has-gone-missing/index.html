The City We Know Has Gone Missing

Seven straight wins gave Manchester City a flying start. Now the old certainty has vanished—and Arsenal is waiting.

Chenzo / 3 min read / November 27, 2026

Over the last decade, Manchester City has made the rest of the Premier League look like it was playing catch-up. Pep’s teams controlled games, smothered opponents, and made winning look almost routine.

That version of City? I don’t see it right now.

Pep is gone. Enzo Maresca took over, and City started with seven straight league wins. At that point, City looked like a title contender. Then football did what football does: it made everyone look silly for getting comfortable. Maresca’s seven straight wins to start his City tenure set a Premier League record. Manchester City’s report on the run

City has now lost three of its last four league matches against teams it should have beaten. Arsenal has taken the lead back, and City has gone from setting the pace to handing the invitation back with the address written on it. Straight-up bullshit, if you ask me.

And the bad run isn’t happening in a vacuum. An independent Premier League commission found City guilty of serious financial-rule breaches across a nine-season period, along with most of the charges concerning the club’s failure to cooperate with the investigation. City has appealed the decision. The commission’s findings and the appeal announcement.

I’m not saying the players have stopped caring. Maybe Maresca needs more time. Maybe opponents have figured out the system. But when there’s a major question mark over the club’s future, it’s fair to wonder whether that uncertainty is getting into the dressing room. I can’t read the players’ minds. I can barely read a City press conference without needing a second coffee.

My heart wants to believe City won’t let its loyal fans down. But Sunday’s trip to Arsenal is a serious test, and sentiment doesn’t win away matches. City needs to show it still has the fight and quality everyone expects.

Right now, I’m taking Arsenal to win at home. City has enough talent to prove me wrong, obviously. That’s what makes this recent run so ridiculous.

Maybe this is just a rough patch. But it feels bigger than that. For the first time in a long time, City looks vulnerable—and this might be the end of an era.
