The Legionaries Aren’t Going Anywhere

After two decades of ball hockey, championships, injuries, and setbacks, Josh King explains why the Legionaries still matter—and why he’s not walking away.

Josh King / 7 min read / November 19, 2026

If you type “Indianapolis Legionaries” into YouTube, you’ll see what might be the worst ball hockey team ever assembled in American history.

A lot of you know how much I loved the Indiana Ball Hockey Association (IBHA) at Ellenberger Park. I was suspended indefinitely this past year, and I’ve spent considerable time considering a defamation lawsuit and other claims. I haven’t filed because I genuinely don’t like being on the offensive side of karma. Still, I’m willing to defend myself. If the president wants to challenge me, I’m willing to let the courts decide who’s right.

For those new to the ball hockey drama, I’ve played this sport for more than 20 years. I’ve been suspended from every rink I’ve ever played at, largely because ball hockey is an incredibly physical sport. For guys who can’t inline or ice skate, it’s often their only opportunity to play hockey. A lot of us came from farming backgrounds, where life back home wasn’t always easy. Hockey was our escape. For many of us, it was all we had.

I originally bought the Legionaries from IBHA and took the team to Pennsylvania. We won three consecutive championships before eventually falling short of another title. We invested in players, bought some of the nicest jerseys around, and watched the franchise fall from one of the higher divisions all the way to the bottom league, where we currently sit in last place.

We might be the oldest team ever assembled. I’m actually the second-youngest guy on the roster. My ball hockey game has fallen off a cliff, and I was getting ready to call it quits. Then I realized something: Whenever I’m playing regularly, I’m in great shape. When I’m not playing, I tend to let myself go.

I want JKSPORTSBOOK to promote healthy habits, and I’ve decided I’m going to keep playing in 2027. I’m also committed to keeping the Indianapolis Legionaries alive for at least another decade.

2027 will be my final season playing in the NBHL. I’ve personally agreed to play for Jacksonville this year, and I’m looking forward to helping the expansion team find its footing.

Here’s a little-known fact: I’ve never won a game in the NBHL.

I started with Indianapolis, where I accounted for 66% of the team’s total goals despite missing half the games. I won the team’s scoring title and, naturally, self-appointed myself MVP.

Then I went to California Tier 2, where I had a goal disallowed, smoked a lot of weed, and got completely messed up on a cannabis pill—I didn’t even know those existed. I was basically benched and never returned. I even paid a third party to review my game footage. They said I played just fine, which probably wasn’t the feedback they would have preferred to give me.

After that, I played in Texas for Oklahoma, where I took a hit to the back that resulted in several broken bones. It completely ruined the experience for me. I tried working my way back, but I was never quite the same player.

Eventually, I started getting back into ball hockey shape, only to get banned from Indiana.

I took six months off to recover. This fall, I started strong, but my game eventually fell off a cliff again. I was ready to walk away from ball hockey and focus exclusively on roller and ice hockey.

Then something unexpected happened. Playing with the older guys reminded me why I fell in love with this sport in the first place. I started enjoying the game again, even while thinking I was playing my final season.

The Legionaries are on YouTube tonight, and I’ll be creating some content since I’m out of the lineup. Our final two regular-season games are Monday night, and I’m 50/50 on whether I’ll suit up.

Moving forward, I’ll share more in-depth ball hockey stories right here on this page. There’s a lot more to this story, but here’s a general synopsis of why the Legionaries mean so much to me.

1. The Scottdale Bears

I played for the Scottdale Bears for over a decade. Before joining Indianapolis, they were my favorite team of all time. That organization will always hold a special place in my heart.

2. The Original Indianapolis Experience

I was genuinely shocked by how bad the roster was when I first joined Indianapolis. Despite that, the Legionaries quickly became special to me. I met a lot of people in Indy whom I still proudly call friends today.

3. The Pennsylvania Magic

The first Legionaries team in Pennsylvania had something special. We had a certain chemistry and a little bit of magic. Some guys resented me for featuring Stu so often, but we were just having fun and enjoying the experience.

4. The NIL Experiment

When I started paying players last year, it became painfully obvious that my NIL experiment had failed. In my view, the players didn’t appreciate the investment. Ironically, the team stopped winning after I started paying them. That Legionaries roster was probably my least favorite of all time.

It was an expensive lesson, but one worth sharing.

5. The Future

The Legionaries aren’t going anywhere. I plan on playing until I’m 65, which means this franchise could be around until at least 2052.

Twenty years of ball hockey, countless teammates, championships, disappointments, suspensions, injuries, and some unbelievable stories. I’ve had my ups and downs with this sport, but I still love it.

The Legionaries will outlive most of the rosters that have worn the jersey. If I have anything to say about it, they’ll still be around long after I’m done playing. This story is far from over.
