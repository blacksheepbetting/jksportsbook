The Teams That Have My Heart

Josh King on the teams that still have his heart—and why a Raiders upset in Denver could put Fernando Mendoza in the conversation.

Josh King / 4 min read / November 19, 2026

The only teams I’m truly a fan of are the Pittsburgh Pirates, the Las Vegas Raiders, and Pitt Panthers football and basketball.

I grew up a huge Penguins fan, but over the years I became more a fan of the league and less attached to Pittsburgh. When they tore down Mellon Arena—the Igloo—it broke the bond I’d carried with the team since childhood. PNC Park, on the other hand, still has that magic.

I was a clubhouse reporter for the Pirates for four years: 2016, 2017, 2018, and 2025. As I’ve gotten older, I’ve realized I enjoy watching games from home more than being at the ballpark.

I watch or listen to almost every Pirates game. More than anything, I want to see them win a World Series.

I do like the Steelers, but my first game in person was against the Oakland Raiders. The first time I saw the Legion of Doom and experienced Raiders fans, I knew I was hooked for life. I love going to road games, and I was there for the game that became Josh McDaniels’ last as head coach.

I’ve been a Raiders fan since they lost the Super Bowl to the Buccaneers, but the JaMarcus Russell years were when I really started watching every game. Tom Cable was the coach, and Bruce Gradkowski shocked the Steelers and the football world. You’ll still catch me watching the Steelers pretty regularly, but there’s nothing quite like a Raiders game.

Some of my biggest winning bets have come from Raiders games.

A lot of people have been asking me about Mendoza. I watched the game against Seattle, and the play calling left me scratching my head. Going into the Denver game, the Raiders are long shots to win, but I genuinely believe an upset could put Mendoza in the conversation with Ben Roethlisberger. Ben was arguably the greatest rookie quarterback we’ve ever seen, winning all 14 of his starts. I don’t think we’ll ever see anything like that again.

I’m not saying Mendoza is the next Ben Roethlisberger or the second coming of Christ. I like the kid, and a lot of people believe he’s special. If he really is, pulling off an upset in Denver could set the tone for his entire career. Raider Nation isn’t easily impressed, so that would certainly get people’s attention.

I also regularly listen to Hondo Carpenter on YouTube. Look him up if you haven’t already; he puts out some great Raiders content.

People have also been asking me about the Cousins trade. I liked it. The Raiders still could have made the playoffs, but for whatever reason, they seem to be clearly punting on those chances—unless they pull off the upset against Denver. If they do, they’ve got a legitimate shot.

The biggest problem is that this team simply doesn’t have enough wide receivers who can make plays. I’ve been screaming about that all year.

As for Coach Kubiak, he was doing a perfect job until halftime. Then he put more pressure on his team by declaring, “WE ARE GOING TO WIN THIS GAME.” Well, they didn’t. I had a lot of money riding on the Raiders and cashed out shortly after that.

Maxx Crosby continues to dominate, and I’m incredibly thankful Baltimore failed him on that physical. The Ravens could end up paying a serious price for that decision, especially if they miss the playoffs.

I also took some heat from podcast listeners because I said I wanted to see Aidan O’Connell get a few starts. Well, I got my wish. Honestly, I would have been perfectly content going into the season with him as the starter. I wanted to see where his development and progression stood.

Mendoza was sacked six times last week. No quarterback can keep taking that kind of punishment and expect to stay healthy. Just look at what happened to David Carr in Houston. You can take only so many hits before they start affecting your career.

As for Jeanty, I think he’ll be fine. The Raiders simply don’t have enough playmakers on the outside, and that’s clearly affecting their running game. Defenses don’t have to respect the passing attack enough, making life much harder for everyone in the backfield.

At the end of the day, I’m a Pirates and Raiders fan through and through. I’ll always appreciate Pittsburgh sports, but those are the teams that truly have my heart.
